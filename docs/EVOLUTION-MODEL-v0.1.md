# Evolution model v0.1

Status: proposed operating model. [Specification](SPECIFICATION-v0.1.md)

| Stage | Useful question | Output | Decision boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Observe | What happened or changed? | Source-linked signal | Collection needs appropriate data access |
| Reflect | What might explain it? | Interpretation, alternatives, uncertainty | Interpretation remains a proposal |
| Hypothesize | What change should produce what result? | Falsifiable hypothesis | Management sets priority and resources |
| Experiment | How can we test it usefully? | Bounded comparison and observations | Engineering enforces permitted effects |
| Validate | What does the evidence support? | Scoped evaluation | Pass, fail, and insufficient stay distinct |
| Adopt | What should we change now? | Management decision and engineering activation | Evidence does not grant authority |
| Monitor | Does it remain suitable? | Review, containment, correction, or retirement | Recovery checks current state and permission |

Learning is iterative. A contradictory result can return to reflection; insufficient evidence can justify another experiment; a harmful effect can require immediate containment. Monitoring does not require that all previous hypotheses be retained forever or that every signal start a new experiment.

## Three depths of change

1. Improve the current practice: for example, make an approval explanation easier to understand.
2. Reconsider the assumptions or objective: for example, discover that reducing replies does not reduce customer effort.
3. Improve the learning method: for example, change a rubric whose scores hide disagreement.

These depths describe what is being questioned. They do not define automatic autonomy levels. The second and third may require Management decisions, new participant involvement, and re-evaluation of earlier results.

## Four coupled views

Review AI task performance, human capability, collaboration, and broader outcomes together. A strong result in one view can coexist with a failure in another. Define tradeoffs explicitly and avoid a single score that lets speed compensate for a critical safety or agency failure.

## Cadence

Use event-driven review for material failures, model changes, policy changes, or contradiction. For an initial team pilot, a short weekly learning review and a review at the end of the experiment are reasonable starting practices; adjust them to actual work. Give every adopted lesson an owner and trigger rather than accumulating unattended retrospectives.

Measure the effort spent collecting, reviewing, and maintaining learning. Combine records and meetings when that keeps the decisions understandable.
