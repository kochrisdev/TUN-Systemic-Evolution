## Next milestones, in order of decision value

| Milestone | Deliverable | Exit evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Name a real learning question | Product/workflow, affected people, owner, baseline, bounded charter | People involved find the question useful; data and participation conditions are agreed |
| 2. Collect real, permitted evidence | Comparable observations and relevant human practice/assessment | Results, adverse findings, missingness, burden, applicability, and uncertainty reported |
| 3. Make a bounded adoption decision | Decision with alternatives, exact change, monitoring, and recovery | Evidence supports the chosen scope; current execution permissions established separately |
| 4. Verify and monitor real effects | Appropriate provider adapter and exercised recovery | Actual activation, outcomes, regression response, residual effects, and participant feedback |
| 5. Generalize only what transfers | Second workflow and versioned adapters | Applicability reviewed; differences documented; full applicable assessment performed |

Technical work needed before effectful production use includes authenticated identities and evidence, cross-store interruption recovery, current-authority enforcement across real boundaries, coordinated recovery, and data lifecycle controls. Prioritize these against the first adopter's actual risks rather than building a universal platform in advance.

## Review the learning method itself

After a cycle, ask: Which decisions changed? What mistakes stopped recurring? What can people now do or explain? Which lessons transferred? How much collection, practice, and review effort was required?

Retire fields and ceremonies that do not improve decisions. Knowledge graphs, multi-agent optimization, training pipelines, and automated experiment proposals should follow demonstrated needs. The desired result is better shared capability and outcomes, not a larger volume of experiments or records.
