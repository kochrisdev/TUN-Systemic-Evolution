# Run your first learning cycle

Status: proposed team practice, not a completed field study. Use this guide with the [experiment charter](../templates/experiment-charter.md). Adjust the duration and rigor to the consequences; a calendar deadline is not an evidence threshold.

## 1. Choose a decision worth improving

Select a recurring difficulty in one workflow. Ask the people doing and receiving the work what better would mean. Name an accountable owner and a question whose answer could change a practice, a product configuration, or an assumption.

Example: “Can evidence-first AI drafts improve support quality while preserving reviewers' error detection and keeping review effort acceptable?” Keep sending authority with the person. Do not introduce new tools, data access, and a new prompt in the same first comparison unless the bundle itself is the intended subject.

## 2. Establish the starting point

Record the current configuration, tasks, participant experience, assistance condition, measurement method, and relevant limits. If the baseline is unknown, the first cycle may simply establish it. Do not relabel an estimate as a measured baseline.

Choose a small set of measures: one intended benefit, the human or safety conditions that must remain acceptable, and the effort needed to achieve it. Note who might be hidden by an average. Ask whether collecting each measure will change a decision.

## 3. Plan the change and human practice

Complete the charter before relying on results. Pin the candidate and case sets, choose a comparison, define sufficient evidence and stopping conditions, and separate practice/tuning cases from assessment cases. Agree participation and permitted data uses before collection.

If people are meant to learn, plan the practice itself: attempt a task, explain reasoning, compare feedback, try a new case, and assess transfer with a declared assistance condition. A changed interface alone is not a learning intervention. See [human–AI co-evolution](HUMAN-AI-COEVOLUTION-v0.1.md).

## 4. Run within the agreed boundary

Obtain the required Management decision and Engineering permissions. Capture observations, uncertainty, participant concerns, and deviations from the plan. Stop or contain the activity under its existing authority when a stop condition occurs. Preserve unresolved effects and follow the relevant incident process.

Do not quietly change a criterion to obtain a passing result. A useful new finding can become a new hypothesis and a new revision.

## 5. Review evidence, then decide

Use the [review template](../templates/evolution-review.md) to distinguish supported, contradicted, and insufficient findings. Consider alternative explanations and human burden. Write a lesson narrow enough for the evidence.

Management records whether to adopt, revise, retain the baseline, or defer. If adopting, identify the exact artifacts, scope, conditions, next review, monitoring owner, and recovery limits. A strong comparison may justify only a limited next trial.

## 6. Verify the change and revisit the lesson

For an effectful change, Engineering authorizes execution and verifies the actual result. Check outcomes afterward; activation success and benefit are different claims. Review the lesson when its population, tools, model, policy, or supporting evidence changes.

At the end, ask: Which decision improved? What can people now do or explain? Which assumption changed? What remains uncertain? Was the learning worth its collection and review cost? Remove unnecessary process while preserving the evidence needed for accountability.

## Minimum useful output

One learning case can hold the question, charter, evidence links, review, decision, and follow-up. The four [templates](README.md) are prompts, not four mandatory forms. A small cycle is complete when its decision and remaining uncertainty are understandable—not when every field contains text.
