# Human–AI co-evolution v0.1

Status: proposed practice; no human study has been run for this draft.

## Two learning tracks and their relationship

For people, use a cycle of setting a learning intention, attempting a task, comparing reasoning and outcomes, receiving feedback, practicing again, and checking transfer. Where retention matters, revisit an unfamiliar task after an appropriate interval. Let participants explain the reasons behind choices and challenge the assessment.

For the AI system, use pinned artifacts, representative cases, independent checks appropriate to the claim, regression cases, and monitored changes. Prompts, retrieval, memory, tools, models, and training have different update mechanisms. Name which one changed.

Study the relationship as well: whether people detect errors, know when to seek help, can override the system, and have the time and information to exercise judgment. Include participant feedback about workload and reliance.

## Example measures

| Claim | Possible observation | Limitation to preserve |
|---|---|---|
| People learned a skill | Performance on unfamiliar tasks with a declared assistance condition | Better assisted output alone cannot show independent competence |
| Learning lasted | Delayed assessment on comparable new cases | Repeated exposure to the same test can inflate results |
| Reliance is better calibrated | Appropriate acceptance and rejection across correct and flawed suggestions | Lower override frequency can mean either improvement or passivity |
| Review became more effective | Detection of planted defects in a controlled exercise | Planted defects may differ from real production errors |
| Work became more sustainable | Review effort, workload accounts, recovery effort | An average can hide a subgroup carrying extra work |

Use the few measures relevant to the claim. For higher-consequence work, obtain appropriate domain and participant review of the method. The framework provides no universal competence score or psychological assessment.

## Participation and data

Define participation, applicable notice or consent, withdrawal, individual access, and escalation before collecting data. Prefer aggregate reporting where individual records are unnecessary. Keep development evidence separate from employment assessment unless a distinct, transparent, authorized process establishes that purpose. Do not infer sensitive traits from interaction patterns.

Human learning cannot be rolled back by restoring a prompt. Changed habits, expectations, or disclosed information may persist. Include coaching, explanation, and recovery support where an adopted change is later withdrawn.

## First field study

Choose one reversible workflow, a small appropriate participant group, and a named owner. Establish an assisted baseline, assess a new candidate, and include a later transfer task where human skill is part of the claim. Prespecify how prior experience, practice effects, differing case difficulty, and missing observations will affect interpretation. Participant burden is part of the cost of the experiment.

The offline pilot's `human_detection` metric is a synthetic aggregate illustrating a constraint. It is not evidence of learning, skill retention, consent, or a completed human study.
