# Introduction

A support team changes an AI drafting prompt. Replies become faster. Reviewers begin missing a category of mistakes because the new wording appears more confident. Has the system improved?

TUN Systemic Evolution gives the team a way to answer. It records the intended benefit, the human and technical baseline, the candidate change, and the conditions that must remain acceptable. It tests the change, records contrary evidence, and makes a decision that can be revisited.

The unit of improvement is the whole working relationship: the person, the AI, their tools, their responsibilities, and the people affected by their work. Better output does not by itself establish better judgment, more appropriate reliance, or better customer outcomes.

Human learning can include practice, reflection, coaching, and delayed assessment on unfamiliar cases. AI improvement can include a changed prompt, retrieval strategy, tool, model, or workflow. Model training changes parameters and needs a separate training and data pipeline. A conversation or saved memory does not prove that model parameters changed.

Management chooses what matters, funds the experiment, and owns the adoption decision. Engineering enforces authority and records actual effects. Design makes evidence and controls usable. Evolution supplies the method for converting an experience into a supported lesson and a monitored change.

Start manually with the [experiment charter](../templates/experiment-charter.md). Select one useful question, one bounded change, one accountable owner, and a small set of measures. Use the [worked example](../examples/human-ai-support-evolution.md) to see why a faster candidate may be rejected and a modest improvement may deserve further testing.

The [offline pilot](PILOT.md) then demonstrates part of the record handling. Its measurements are synthetic. Real benefits require observations from real work.
